## A method for finding the interesting part

I can make this explicit enough to discuss with a human editor. This is our proposed method, informed by the sources below and your preference for asking why:

1. **Start with something particular.** A strange layout, a recurring custom, a choice, an amusing incident or a visible change. State what first attracted attention in one ordinary sentence.
2. **Ask why it took this form.** What did people want, what constrained them, and what did their choices produce? For a light anecdote, the attraction may be character or comic timing rather than a large causal explanation.
3. **Find what makes this account distinctive.** Identify the detail, relationship or reversal that changes the first impression. Compare a plausible alternative explanation so research can overturn our pitch.
4. **Check that the promise exists in the material.** Point to a passage, image, record or contemporary observation. Name one targeted follow-up that might make the story better or show it is weak. Researchers can initiate this, and writers or the lead can request it.
5. **Choose the form and position.** Explanation, choice/consequence, transformation, portrait/scene, or connection/pattern; perhaps simply an aside. Decide whether the subject benefits from standing here, travelling through the area, or being paired with another encounter.
6. **Pitch it to the editor.** In roughly a paragraph: the attraction; what we discovered; the key detail; how the listener encounters it; and the question that could change our choice. Explain why it is worth the listener's time without promising an emotion or manufacturing stakes.

Then Sidi can choose **develop, combine, hold or drop** and explain the decisive reason. We keep that reason and one rejected alternative. This records usable editorial judgement without treating an agent's private reasoning as training data or imposing a numerical interest score.

Research can deepen or enrich a story after its basic claims are supported. The limit is relevance: give each further search a question and an expected benefit, review what it returned, and stop when it no longer changes the account or supplies worthwhile texture. If repeated searches return the same derivative material, switch source family, narrow the story or return to the editor. No source-count minimum or endless requirement to disprove every possible explanation.

99PI's own [pitch guidance](https://99percentinvisible.org/about/pitch/) asks for a revealing story about an overlooked aspect of the world, with an initial narrative arc that can change during editing. It also describes further research for promising but incomplete pitches. That supports a development stage, rather than expecting a first pitch to contain a finished script. Its subject restrictions are specific to its programme, not rules for our tours.

I also revisited [Ira Glass's account of finding ideas](https://transom.org/2004/ira-glass/). The useful move is to pursue possible meanings through concrete questions, accept that some leads fail, and distinguish a real attraction from something that merely resembles a respectable radio subject. Our method above adapts those practices; it does not require every anecdote to carry a profound conclusion.

## Survey broadly and then assemble

The [survey protocol](AREA-TOUR-SURVEY.md#bounded-search-programme-for-the-next-area) now has a proposed bounded search programme, including dense visitor districts and sparsely documented areas. It produces enough alternatives for routing and editorial selection together, without deeply researching every entry.

There are three different objects:

| Object | What it contains | Who develops it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place or story candidate | A place can have several stories; a story can span several places | Survey discovers it; research develops the substance |
| Curated shortlist | Candidates that appear worth our time, with relative strengths and drawbacks | Curator working to Sidi's brief |
| Tour assembly | A sequence of stops, narrated legs and quiet, with route costs and a developing experience | Assembly function working with route review and the curator |

**Assembly is a useful separate function.** It need not be another permanent agent or a new system. In the workshop it should produce two materially different outlines from the same pool and explain the tradeoffs. The same agent can curate and assemble if that works; keeping the two decisions visible lets us detect whether an attractive theme is distorting the route or a convenient route is wasting the stories.

Research may suggest whole-tour themes, but these are hypotheses. Assembly can discover a better thread, or a loose portrait, after seeing the candidates together. A thin destination story may work well on a narrated leg; walking chapters also deserve substantial subjects selected for that format from the outset.

## Confidence and imagination

I agree that historical reasoning should not collapse into “we only have an advert”. A contemporary advert is meaningful evidence. An occurrence may be a reasonable inference where the context supports it and nothing material contradicts it; record that basis without automatically burdening the narration with a disclaimer. This is different from claiming independent confirmation we do not have.

Likewise, documented behaviour from the same period can inform an imagined scene. My earlier wording was too categorical about making that leap. My proposed boundary is about what the wording claims: a period performance can supply plausible activity in a reconstruction, while a distinctive stunt known from another event should not silently become a documented incident at this venue. We can vividly describe the kind of performance or tell the known incident at its actual setting. Invention is also available as invention, rather than manufactured archival evidence.

Use qualitative editorial judgements tied to reasons: directly supported, reasonably inferred, reconstructed, or unresolved/disputed. Adjust the detail, wording or inclusion accordingly. These are proposed working labels, not new package-schema states or calibrated probabilities. Your request for confidence thresholds does not require invented percentages.

The Cardiff/Miller point also needed a better explanation. Their [own description](https://cardiffmiller.com/walks/) discusses blending a fictional world with real surroundings. There is no general reason we cannot learn from or explore that. The listener should understand whether they are hearing historical explanation, reconstruction or an invented scene. Disclosure once at a sensible transition can preserve both trust and immersion. Literal route instructions still need to remain unambiguous. In the follow-up discussion, Sidi chose **“Start with reconstruction and atmosphere”**. That is the next writing brief; invented dialogue and composite characters are not part of this first experiment. It is not a permanent rejection of fictional forms.

## Voice and editorial values

The app can express your editorial perspective. The AI narrator does not need invented personal tastes, memories or lived experience to do that. Warmth can come from attentive explanation, humour, humane interpretation and the accounts of people who were there. Transparent AI provenance is a product direction; it does not by itself establish factual accuracy.

Your example values are starting questions, not a chosen manifesto. My proposal is to collect decisions from the next workshop before fixing the wording. For each value we consider, ask what it would make us include, how it would change the telling, and what counterexample it must allow. “Shared cultural life matters”, for instance, can motivate investigation without proving why particular residents campaigned for a venue. We can have a viewpoint and still let evidence complicate it.

## Books and visual route review

**Books:** yes, a small comparative study would be useful. Start with two or three contrasting guides already available to us, using a complete representative walk and the introduction from each. Ask a bounded analyst to record selection, stop/leg balance, context, sentence rhythm, humour, navigation and endings, with page references and counterexamples. Then compare the patterns with our brief. Study the actual text before making claims about its writing. There is no need to buy a large library or build a corpus first, and none has been acquired or sent to another service in this review.

**Street imagery:** make visual route review an explicit bounded task. For a shortlisted ambiguity, inspect the incoming approach, intended standing view and relevant detail; record what is visible, image date and whether it changes the instruction. Small details can work if the listener receives a clear locator and enough time. Start with a few consequential questions; deduplicate views and stop after they are resolved. Later automation should have per-question and per-tour request limits, a retry limit and an approved cost budget. Google's [Static API policy](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/policies) restricts imagery storage and exempts panorama IDs; do not assume an API can supply a permanently cached screenshot library. A concrete provider/mechanism review belongs with that integration, not a new prerequisite for this discussion.
