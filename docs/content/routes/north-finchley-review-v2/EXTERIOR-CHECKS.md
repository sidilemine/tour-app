## B3 — Finchley Quaker Meeting House, 58 Alexandra Grove

### Evidence and actual observations

- [Finchley Quaker Meeting’s own website](https://finchley.quakermeeting.org/) identifies 58 Alexandra Grove, N12 8HG, and links its wall artwork. The [first-person commission account](https://northfinchleytowncentre.co.uk/local-stories/story/wall-sculpture-at-finchley-meeting-house-message-from-a-bottle/) expressly invites people to see the front-wall work. This establishes the intended public-facing subject; it does not by itself establish unrestricted access to the grounds.
- The article’s [frontage photograph](https://northfinchleytowncentre.co.uk/app/uploads/2022/11/FMH-scaled.jpg) was opened in the browser and visually inspected. The bottle/slate forms occupy the brick wall below a large **Quaker Meeting House** sign, between tall white-framed windows. A green ramp railing crosses the lower foreground. This close photograph alone does not establish the pavement sightline. Its capture date is not supplied; the URL’s upload path is not treated as a capture date.
- [Google Street View, September 2024](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=uDiIQtq7wa1opZ4fEdc1NA&heading=24), panorama ID `uDiIQtq7wa1opZ4fEdc1NA`, camera **51.6131247, -0.1847660**: the front wall, sign and sculpture are visibly identifiable from Alexandra Grove. The low building is set behind a forecourt. A boundary wall and hedge partly screen the lower frontage; an open driveway provides the clearest line towards the artwork. Paving and a dropped-kerb driveway apron are visible along the street. Parked cars occupy part of the forecourt. This is a street panorama, not an indoor or private-ground viewpoint.
- [Google’s institution listing](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Finchley+Quaker+Meeting+58+Alexandra+Grove/) resolves its pin to **51.6132768, -0.1845902**. That is a third-party building/place pin, not the public stopping area. The road setback makes the earlier approximately 28-metre landmark-to-route snap plausible; it does not establish a routing error.

### Practical placement

**Approximate desk nominee: 51.61315, -0.18470**, on the building-side pavement beside the driveway, looking towards the low brick building and its sign. This is roughly 6 metres northeast of the street camera and 8–9 metres from the supplied route end **51.613084, -0.184639**. The offset is a reasoned estimate from visible road/forecourt geometry, not a measured kerb survey. Retain the exact camera coordinate as evidence separately.

Arrival wording should identify **58 Alexandra Grove and the Quaker Meeting House sign**, then ask the visitor to stop on the pavement beside, rather than in, the driveway. No entry, open gate, worship session or access ramp is required. Do not use the building pin as a standing instruction.

**Remaining uncertainty / fallback:** parked vehicles or new vegetation may affect the lower sculpture sightline. ## B4 — Moss Hall Crescent

### Evidence and actual observations

- [Barnet’s conservation-area description](https://www.barnet.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/conservation-and-heritage/conservation-areas/moss-hall-crescent) locates the villas west of Ballards Lane behind a planted strip. Its [2015 appraisal](https://open.barnet.gov.uk/download/2nx73/e21/2015-08-26%20-%20MossHallCrescentConservationArea%20-%20CharacterAppraisalAndManagementProposals.pdf), printed page 14, section 4.2, distinguishes views along the Crescent from partial views through trees. This supports an ensemble story, not a promised uninterrupted panorama of every house. PDF text was inspected; the web screenshot endpoint failed, so PDF photographs are not claimed as visually checked in this assignment.
- Google’s map and satellite presentation were visually inspected: **the Crescent roadway lies between the houses and the planted strip; Ballards Lane lies beyond the strip**. This distinction matters for directions. The grass is not the walking line.
- [Google Street View, April 2022, closer panorama](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=-LwpIuU-o-AonTnOa_W9AA&heading=300), ID `-LwpIuU-o-AonTnOa_W9AA`, camera **51.6102078, -0.1811706**, about 8 metres from the proposed marker **51.61014, -0.18115**. Facing northwest, several pale-brick villa fronts, white window surrounds, roofs, garden walls and gates are visible. The house-side public footway is clearly visible in front of those boundaries. It includes trees/tree pits and driveways; the image does not justify a claim of unlimited pavement width.
- [Adjacent April 2022 panorama](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=rSHdVl8rvmbZNhTVYCIj_w&heading=90), ID `rSHdVl8rvmbZNhTVYCIj_w`, camera **51.6102742, -0.1810717**, was rotated to inspect both sides. The green is across the Crescent road from the house-side pavement. Its edge against the Crescent has a kerb, with no continuous paved walking strip visible there. Planting screens some façades, but the houses and their relationship to the green are legible from the street.

### Practical placement

**Approximate desk nominee: 51.61023, -0.18122**, around 4 metres northwest of the closer street camera, on the house-side pavement. This is approximately 11 metres from the original proposed marker; do not force the nominee within an arbitrary 10-metre radius if doing so would place it in the road. The final route should follow the house-side pavement and treat any centreline routing snap as an approximation.

Arrival wording should identify the **pavement beside the villa garden walls on Moss Hall Crescent**, with the shared green across the small Crescent road. A viewer can appreciate the façades and then the larger setting without entering a front garden or walking onto the grass. The script’s historical shared-green discussion remains appropriate despite partial screening.

**Remaining uncertainty / fallback:** foliage and parked vehicles vary, and the imagery predates this walk. Choose a clear piece of the same footway away from a tree pit or driveway; no particular window, doorway or single-house view is essential. The existing narration concerns the ensemble and its original arrangement. No separate physical check is necessary before ordinary use.
