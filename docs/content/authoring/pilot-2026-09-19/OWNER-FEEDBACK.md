## Decisions and their meaning

| Point | Owner direction / reaction | Consequence for future authoring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose of evocation | First identify what the listener cannot readily picture from ordinary experience; spend descriptive time revealing that. | A scene needs a particular contribution to understanding. Plausibility and sensory language alone do not justify it. This refines the positive direction to reconstruct vanished life; it does not retreat into bare facts. |
| Gaumont | The wood panelling begins well, but the visit's distinctive historical experience is underdeveloped. | Research what a visit here, in its period, involved before choosing further colour. The owner's expectation of difference is a research question, not proof of a particular programme, ritual or audience. |
| School | Inclusion and a vignette are welcome; opening books and moving chairs feel universal and waste the opportunity. | Preserve the subject's potential. Seek something revealing about that education, institution or period; if none is supportable, state the discovery briefly instead of expanding generic activity. |
| artsdepot balance | The explanation improved, but repeated intentions displaced the vivid protest/masks/Madness material. | Compare a revision with the strongest earlier material. Let supported actions carry some of the meaning, and shorten repeated explanation to make room. No obligation to preserve every old anecdote. |
| artsdepot ending | **Leaning towards the warmer ending, conditional on better writing.** | A direction for another draft, not approval of the existing wording or of warm endings for every stop. |
| Duration | About two minutes feels somewhat long but acceptable for some especially important or interesting stops. | Use duration selectively according to value; neither a default length nor a universal cap. |
| Alexandra Grove opening | **Prefers the railway-led alternative** because it supplies context and a proper introduction. | Supersedes the coordinator's street-led recommendation for this sample. The preference does not establish that transport is the best opening everywhere. |
| Missing before-state | Needs pre-rail journey time and what occupied the land before housing. | Identify the comparison and prior scene that make a change meaningful. A standalone improved journey time and “an estate” leave relevant knowledge gaps. |

## What the retained material shows

The [original artsdepot passage](BASELINE.json) included masks, banners and Madness; [P01-C05 and the campaign-colour note](P01-RESEARCH.md) retained those as usable material with source limits. The completed draft omitted them. The failure therefore includes selection and integration, rather than simply a researcher failing to discover the story. The new explanation was useful to Sidi; preserve that gain while improving the balance.

The older script and saved research support a reported Madness appearance through artsdepot's institutional history. The owner's word **“free”** is preserved above, but the inspected local handoff does not separately establish admission terms. Verify that detail before using it as a researched claim. Also retain the council-account qualification: the 2002 alternative concerned a joint arts/library facility instead of a theatre, so restoring the campaign must not restore the older simplified either/or account.

The [walking research](P02-RESEARCH-ROUTE.md) supplies the half-hour rail journey, estate subdivision and an older houses-and-fields context for Nether Street. It does not supply a comparable pre-rail journey time or establish the exact prior land use along every part of Alexandra Grove. Do not turn regional rural context into a surveyed description of this street. The relevant prior journey needs an origin, destination, mode and period; otherwise a precise-looking before/after ratio would mislead.

## Proposed application at the next drafting task

These are the engineer's practical implementation of the feedback, not additional owner-approved quotas:

1. **Before researching a scene, name the missing picture.** What would a listener otherwise imagine incorrectly, generically or not at all? Return the particular evidence that improves that picture. Period context can support reconstruction without a source for every ordinary gesture.
2. **Before writing a transformation, name the missing comparison.** What was here, how did people do this before, and what actually changed? Research only the gaps that make the chosen story intelligible; avoid an exhaustive preamble.
3. **Before accepting a revision, compare gains and losses.** Read the earlier strong material beside the new draft. Identify useful explanation gained, distinctive action lost, and repetition that could pay for its return. Review all supplied alternatives in context.
4. **Review the actual passage against those answers.** The previous briefs already asked for distinctive detail and what differed from today. The pilot did not apply that standard strongly enough; another instruction alone is not evidence of improvement. A factual pass and a complete set of agent outputs do not establish an engaging story.

Useful future research questions: what made a Gaumont visit of that period different from a familiar cinema visit; what distinguished the educational setting beyond universal classroom actions; what comparable journey preceded the train; what occupied the housing land; and what exactly the campaign account supports. This capture commissions no new research run, rewritten recording or field session. Current authoring guidance and role briefs now carry these lessons into the next assignment.
