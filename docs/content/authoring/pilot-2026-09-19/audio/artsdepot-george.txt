Here on Nether Street, artsdepot occupies the site of the Gaumont cinema, which opened in 1937.

Picture its auditorium before a film. Elaborate ceiling lights shine above mahogany wall panels; conversation settles as the lights dim. There was a café too, and a stage equipped for live performance. A night here could mean more than the picture on the screen. The cinema closed in 1980, and the building came down seven years later.

The idea that eventually took its place began with a request for more room. At a local consultation in 1996, people asked for a larger version of the Bull Arts Centre in High Barnet. They already had an example of what an arts centre could offer. Getting this one built took another eight years.

Just before artsdepot opened in 2004, its director, Alison Duthie, explained the plan to the Finchley Society. Visiting theatre productions would share the building with space for educational and local groups. There would be exhibitions, workshops and courses. The café and bar, she hoped, would make it a place to meet. The ambition extended from the special evening out to the activities that could bring people back week after week.

That is easier to picture through one small part of today's programme. Creative Circle is a writing group for people over fifty-five, developing poetry and prose together. Earlier this year, artsdepot advertised a performance made by members of that group, its Dance Company and other local artists. Work developed together had a place on the programme alongside visiting shows.

A performance needs an audience, of course. But the rooms between performances matter too: somewhere to try a line of poetry, hear what someone else has made, and return with another draft. From this pavement, the building holds all those possibilities out of sight. An evening out can begin with somebody else's story and lead to a place for your own.
