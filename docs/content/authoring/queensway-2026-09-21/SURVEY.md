# Queensway evening demo: public walk and guide survey

**Accessed 21 September 2026. Status: bounded discovery completed; selection, route and scripts not decided by this survey.**

## Brief and boundaries

Same-day evening demo near Queensway station and 46 Queensborough Terrace: three stops, four at most, **one** narrated walking story, George. The owner selected **20–25 minutes**. This is a deliberately compact dinner-adjacent demo, not a new length rule for finished tours. Core patch: Queensway, Whiteleys, Leinster/Craven Hill Gardens and Moscow Road; nearby comparisons: Paddington, Lancaster Gate and Notting Hill. No dependence on Kensington Gardens gates, paid interiors or private garden access.

Applied [RES-001/002, CUR-001/002 and LEAD-004](../../../ISSUES.md): survey before choosing; pursue useful questions; distinguish published inclusion from verified facts/access; preserve alternatives; budget the real walk rather than summing walking and simultaneous audio twice. - Broad queries combined Bayswater / Queensway / Leinster Gardens / Whiteleys with walking tour, guide, self-guided, PDF and evening. Targeted checks covered Westminster Guides, London Walks, VoiceMap, transport heritage, current Queens and Whiteley operators, and St Sophia.
- **Operators/individual guides:** two distinct Westminster offerings; the HappeningNext copy of *Bayswater Revealed* was deduplicated to the originator. Hitchcock reseller summaries were peripheral and not treated as additional local itineraries.
- **Self-guided/app/blog/report:** W2 full text, STQRY list plus relevant preview, RGS viewpoint, dated evening walking-club report. No relevant VoiceMap listing surfaced in the bounded query; that is a search gap, not proof none exists. No audio or video was watched/listened to in this survey.
- **Published guides / civic / specialist:** relevant Liddell PDF segment, RGS viewpoint, TfL sightseeing publication and official venue histories. London Toolkit's Kensington walk starts at Queensway but goes through the park; its full itinerary was not analysed. A London Gardens Trust 2026 walk surfaced but its distant garden-opening programme was not researched for tonight. No paid or printed walking book was bought/read. The LTM library's *Building the Inner Circle railway* (1946) is only a catalogue lead.
- **Current places:** primary pages for Queens, Whiteley and St Sophia checked below. Estate-agent guides and resident/council regeneration reports surfaced; promotional “now open” wording and old completion forecasts were not adopted. Detailed present trading/opening status belongs to focused research if selected.

## Candidate pool and assembly options

These are editorial possibilities, **not ordered itineraries or owner selections**. Route feasibility, standing positions, crossings, visibility after dusk and measured audio must determine the final assembly.

| Candidate | Potential payoff | Main limitation / question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **23–24 Leinster Gardens, front** | A visible puzzle that opens into steam-era engineering and what a respectable street concealed. Several independent publishers feature it. | Front details may be harder after dusk; distinguish road/pavement from private forecourts. No rear-wall climbing or promised train view. |
| **Whiteleys / The Whiteley, Queensway frontage** | A building designed to make shopping an event, now changing purpose; strong scale and present-day contrast. | Keep the 1863 business separate from the 1911 building. A retained façade does not mean the old public store survives inside. Current occupancy/access requires verification. |
| **Queens rink, Queensway entrance** | The least grand doorway can conceal the most unexpected activity. Different tone and genuine long-lived leisure. | Street view reveals the entrance, not the rink; narration must explain that honestly. Avoid an unsupported celebrity roll-call. |
| **St Sophia, Moscow Road exterior** | Why Greek merchant families commissioned a Byzantine church here; evidence names the organisers and funding mechanism. | Stronger community story, but closed Monday evening under ordinary published hours. Do not promise mosaics/interior or audible worship. |
| **Skanderbeg, Porchester Gardens** | Compact potential surprise about diaspora and remembrance. | First establish why this memorial is here and what it commemorates; do not substitute a generic heroic biography for the local link. |
| **Hallfield Estate edge** | Architectural contrast: planned post-war homes versus surrounding terraces. | More route cost and a less self-evident dusk viewing position. Not needed merely to fill a fourth stop. |
| **Former Queens Cinema façade** | Another surviving frontage behind changed use. | Risks repetition with Whiteleys and extra distance. Current form and meaningful story remain unresearched. |

**Option A — appearances and hidden activity:** false-house front, Whiteley frontage and Queens rink entrance. Their mechanisms differ: conceal the railway, display the emporium, conceal leisure below street level. A walking story could investigate how the district sold convenience and urban living; first identify a supported scene rather than merely repeating the three stops' facts.

**Option B — making a home in Bayswater:** false-house front, Whiteley frontage and St Sophia exterior. A walking story could give the terraces and changing population context. More human/community contrast; greater risk of a long cross-neighbourhood leg for a dinner demo. Do not turn all migration into one undifferentiated story.

**Tighter fallback:** a Queensway-centred cluster might combine rink, Whiteley and the Skanderbeg garden only if the memorial's local purpose and timing justify it. Do not force four stops; an excellent three-stop demo is the brief. No final chapter position is implied here.

| [Whiteley history](https://www.thewhiteleylondon.com/history), Q-P04 | Small Westbourne Grove draper in 1863; Queensway store opened 2 November 1911. | Developer retrospective, including promotional/folkloric embellishment; it does not make the elephant anecdote or every superlative firm history. |
| [St Sophia history](https://www.stsophia.org.uk/history/), Q-P05; [current site](https://www.stsophia.org.uk/), Q-P06 | Merchant-family relocation explains the commission; communal voluntary contributions, 1879 first liturgy and 1882 consecration are distinguished. Further cautions: STQRY's 1912 Whiteleys date conflicts with the operator's precise 1911 opening; its Hitler-headquarters claim remains unverified. General descriptions of Bayswater's affluent terraces, cosmopolitan life or railway growth need concrete actors and evidence before becoming our walking chapter. An old guide's shops, personal favourites and present tense are not 2026 observations.
