## Research and its limits

The [research review](../WALKING-TOUR-DESIGN-RESEARCH.md) retains publication details, original links, what was actually read and limitations. This synthesis does not claim a new literature search or a universal theory of tour quality.

| Input | Application | Limit retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stern and Powell 2013 | Organisation, theme and interested delivery | Observational work on live guides; no measured synthetic-voice effect |
| Wolf and colleagues 2013 | Balance discovery with relaxed walking and social space | Different media and park setting; no universal speech/silence ratio |
| Beattie and Schneider 2018 | Respect differing visitor interests and control | Self-selected interpretive resources; no causal proof that guides lower satisfaction |
| The Rough Mile 2017 | Audio can change perception of a familiar street | Small exploratory fictional-audio study; no licence to invent history |
| Ludwig, Heritage Fund and ICOMOS | Place, meaningful ideas, clear language, sources and perspectives | Professional conventions, not experimental proof of a script recipe |
| VoiceMap producer guidance | Route/story timing, useful directions, writing for the ear | Useful practice adapted to this product; typical lengths are not owner requirements |

Tilden and Ham remain further reading identified by the original review, not books claimed to have been fully read. Source scope is preserved rather than upgraded through repeated summaries.

## Corrections that must survive the synthesis

- Short tests do not imply short finished tours; variable spacing supersedes a roughly equal-spacing default.
- Imagined scenes are positively wanted when research supports them. Caution about a memorial's asserted meaning does not forbid historical colour.
- Present-day relevance is required even when the visible condition is decline. A positive-only coda is superseded.
- Broader thought can belong within the same story without a special theme licence. Time caps matter; the proposed percentage is not agreed.
- Names, dates and source counts are not ends in themselves. Research should change the explanation and the scene; facts must be placed where the listener can use them.
- Source coverage, inferred relationships, actual pedestrian access and audible technical behaviour are different kinds of evidence. Success in one does not certify the others.
