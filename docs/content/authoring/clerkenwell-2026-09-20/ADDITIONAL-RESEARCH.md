## Charterhouse: a possible quieter opening contrast

**Reason to consider it:** the square and surviving frontage could reward a visit in their own right, while a living residential institution provides a contrast to the workplaces ahead. The story could ask what a home offered when earning a living was no longer possible. This must earn its route cost; adding an institutional chronology merely to reach 75–90 minutes would weaken the walk.

**Candidate selection:** explain what an almshouse is; identify Sutton as purchaser and funder, and the governors as the people who set admission rules; contrast that selective historical provision with the present residential community. Keep the monastery/mansion sequence to bearings, with no parade of royal guests. The square's burial history and executions would pull this particular tour towards solemn material and are not proposed as its centre. A different, more visual account of monastic daily life remains an alternative, not another complete episode to append.

### Sources and short supporting passages

- **`A-S01` The Charterhouse, [History](https://thecharterhouse.org/explore-the-charterhouse/history/).** Institutional history; undated page. Supporting phrases: “Sutton purchased Howard House in 1611”; “They set the rules for admission”; “people aged 60 and over”. Claims above paraphrase the relevant sections; no primary will or governors' minutes were read.
- **`A-S02` The Charterhouse, [Plan your visit](https://thecharterhouse.org/visit-us/).** Current operator listing. Short passage: “Tuesday to Saturday 10.30am to 4.30pm FREE, no booking required”. This applies to museum display, chapel and shop. Square access is separately listed Tuesday–Sunday, early morning to dusk. Entrance is on the north side near the Malmaison; public rooms do not imply access to the whole residential estate. Occasional closures are expressly possible. Paid 60/90-minute guided tours would extend the outing substantially and are not part of this proposal.
- **`A-S03` London Museum, [A history of the London Charterhouse](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/history-london-charterhouse/).** Museum historical interpretation, undated page. Supporting phrase: “served to them through a hatch in their cell door”. Useful independent synthesis for the monastic alternative and continued almshouse; not evidence that the relevant architectural feature can be seen on this route.

**Practical implication:** the main narration would work from a reviewed public exterior. A brief free museum/chapel visit can be optional when open; the route's timing must state whether it includes that visit. Do not silently count a closed garden or optional interior as guaranteed looking time. Recheck current opening/closure information near the actual outing date.
