# Where to make the next complete tour

20 September 2026. Preliminary discovery for an owner choice, before a full [public-tour survey](../../AREA-TOUR-SURVEY.md). These are three promising areas, not selected itineraries or claims of comprehensive coverage. Public tour descriptions establish what others offer; their historical statements still need independent research before our narration.

**Owner decision, 20 September:** Sidi selected **Clerkenwell / Farringdon**. The comparison below is retained as the basis for that choice. The [area survey](../../survey/clerkenwell-2026-09-20/README.md) is the next handoff; no particular theme, stop sequence or tour length was selected by choosing the area.

## Recommendation

**Clerkenwell first; Hampstead as the strongest alternative.** Clerkenwell offers a useful change from Finchley: a compact part of central London with several kinds of history competing for attention. It should exercise our selection method and the connection between local detail and broader ideas. This is an editorial judgement from the initial discovery below, not a route already measured or approved.

| Area | Why it could make a good next tour | What would need care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clerkenwell / Farringdon** | Existing walks uncover making, printing, political organisation, religion and changing uses of buildings. There is room for both intimate stories and a larger developing question. | Choose a manageable part of the area rather than append every neighbouring attraction. Avoid a parade of famous names. Find suitable stretches for two walking chapters and check current works, crossings and places to stand. |
| **Hampstead village and the Heath edge** | An appealing mix of houses, landscape and public life. Existing walks offer architectural and literary material alongside conservation and ecology, giving us options beyond a famous-residents tour. | Steep sections and Heath surfaces require a deliberate route choice. Interior access must be optional unless explicitly planned. Keep the broader stories rooted in visible surroundings. |
| **Spitalfields / Brick Lane** | Public routes connect work, migration, faith, markets and cultural life. Particularly promising for the owner's request to connect history to what a place is like now. | Avoid presenting communities as tidy successive replacements. Research current voices and conditions. Busy streets and markets need careful listening positions and may constrain walking narration. |

All three offer station-based starts or finishes in published walks. We can design convenient transport endpoints after selecting the area; no travel time or final walk length is promised here. Hampstead is on the Northern line's Edgware branch, so it is not a direct southbound journey from the High Barnet branch. Angel, also on the Northern line, is a possible approach to the wider Clerkenwell area, subject to route selection. See [TfL's Northern line stations](https://tfl.gov.uk/tube/route/northern). Check the actual journey when an outing is scheduled.

## After the choice

1. Complete the broad public-tour survey for that area: council/heritage walks, local societies, commercial and specialist operators, self-guided/audio routes, and relevant community perspectives. Catalogue stops, themes and stories; deduplicate shared sources and record gaps.
2. Research the strongest candidates and present a small number of coherent tour options. Keep the owner's editorial role meaningful before committing to a full script.
3. Develop the route and two narrated walking legs together. Verify the actual approach, viewpoint and awkward turns using maps and available dated street imagery; do not make pedestrian instructions out of prose alone.
4. Extend the existing offline map coverage for the chosen area as a bounded technical change, then prepare and check the package before requesting the phone. The current import contract is tied to North Finchley; see the [M2 closure review](../../../test-results/M2-closure-review-2026-09-20.md).

The second new tour can use a contrasting area or treatment after the first provides useful feedback. No two-area production commitment or fixed number of outings is implied.
