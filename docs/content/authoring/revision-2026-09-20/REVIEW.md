## What works, and what remains a judgement

**Strongest moment:** the organist rising at the keyboard. The auditorium's scale, moving console and two-film bill supply a recognisably different cinema visit. The opening invitation adequately frames the reconstruction; it needs no added spoken disclaimer. The actual mechanism and opening programme are supported by the [specialist cinema account](https://cinematreasures.org/theaters/29620).

The restored masks, banners and Madness appearance give the campaign action and a little humour. The brief institutional attribution is appropriate, “free” is correctly omitted, and the joint arts/library alternative preserves the theatre distinction in the [council account](https://barnet.moderngov.co.uk/Data/Cabinet%20-%202014/201204041900/Agenda/Document%2010.pdf). The text does not claim that protest alone caused the decision.

The present writing group and dated programme give the ending a foundation. “Programme featured” remains within the event listing's evidence. The final possibility—somebody's writing becoming somebody else's evening out—is warmer and more particular than a general claim about community. It also returns naturally to the opening audience. This is a promising answer to Sidi's conditional preference, not his acceptance of the ending.

The strongest omitted earlier material is the café/meeting-place aspiration and the sense of returning week after week. Losing it is justified: the writing group now carries participation concretely, while the restored campaign earns the time. The earlier explanation of professional and local uses survives. No further cinema-programme detail is needed for this version.

### Alexandra Grove

The opening names **Church End** and **Whetstone** within their respective sentences. On a single text reading, the different examples register; no same-trip saving or ratio is asserted. It answers why the half-hour figure matters while keeping the preferred railway-led introduction. Actual delivery should preserve the sentence break and place-name emphasis. The comparison is regional; precise matched endpoints remain unestablished.

The red-brick country house, grounds and subsequent plots now supply the missing landscape. The wording describes the hall and grounds surviving alongside development; it does not pass the later map off as a pre-1867 survey or invent farming beneath individual houses. That assessment uses the researcher’s recorded inspection of the appraisal and map, not a new visual verification here.

The school passage has a distinct educational point. Individual piano lessons alone would be familiar; the surrounding expectation **regardless of taste**, time devoted to accomplishments, and newly available academic future give them their period meaning. “In many middle-class girls' schools” moves clearly into general context before “Picture”, avoiding an invented curriculum for Miss Amner. The [1868 inquiry](https://www.education-uk.org/documents/taunton1868/taunton1.html), pp. 550–553, supports that tension and acknowledges stronger academic teaching. The [University of London timeline](https://www.london.ac.uk/about/history) supports the 1878 opening of full degrees. Neither source establishes this college's university preparation, and the script makes no such claim.

Transport, estate subdivision and the directory entry lead into one another without claiming that the railway caused educational reform. The brief return to learning behind residential addresses closes the excursion before navigation. The older “suburb arrived before the old house left” idea survives in substance; losing the demolition date and lease explanation makes room for the missing landscape and educational purpose. The piano scene remains compressed, but it is no longer generic lesson preparation. Expand it only if listening reveals a specific gap.
