## Recommended material and what it contributes

**Provisional promise:** this corner has offered a place to encounter entertainment and make it together; the newer building's particular offer was something people actively defended.

Use a short cinema scene, the gap between buildings, the contested theatre plan and visible campaign actions, then Duthie's concrete opening offer and one present connection. The organ supplies movement; the campaign supplies people doing something. Those can replace repeated statements about community and ambition. Keep the 1996 origin to a brief bridge if needed, using P01-C03's larger-Bull-Arts-Centre qualification.

The cinema's missing picture is **the scale and composition of the visit**: a large shared auditorium, a mixed film programme and live organ entertainment. Ordinary light-dimming and excitement add little. The best compact combination is the large hall plus the rising console and two films. A broader period alternative is entering a continuously repeating programme part-way through and leaving when it comes round again. Choose between them: explaining both risks making the Gaumont a second full story.

Allocate roughly a quarter of the spoken passage to cinema colour, leaving the centre's campaign and uses room to develop. This is an editorial starting point, not measured audio or a new quota. The warmth can come from the particular opportunity to share one's own work; another abstract sentence about bringing people together is unnecessary.

## Inspectable evidence

### R01 — the local cinema

**Ken Roe, “Gaumont Finchley,” Cinema Treasures, undated contributed history.** [Overview](https://cinematreasures.org/theaters/29620), opening and auditorium paragraphs. Same source as P01-S02, independently reopened.

Minimal passages: “located on a lift on the left side of the orchestra pit”; “guest organist for a week”.

The account also records an equipped stage and café/restaurant. This prevents a simplistic transition from wholly passive filmgoing to participation. Do not narrate the organ as silent-film accompaniment: the 1937 bill was already in the sound era. No opening souvenir or contemporary local advert was successfully inspected, so AR-C01–03 retain specialist-secondary status. The guest organist is useful evidence behind the scene; his name need not consume spoken time.

### R02 — a remembered local action

**Dallas–Pierce–Quintero and Stephen Barrett studio, *North Finchley Town Centre Cultural Action Plan*, 2022.** [PDF, spread 46–47 / PDF page 24](https://northfinchleytowncentre.co.uk/app/uploads/2022/08/NFTC_Cultural_Action_Plan.pdf#page=24), “Spotlight on a local heritage.” Cover extraction contains both August and December 2022; do not infer publication month from the upload path.

Minimal passage: “I used to be fascinated by the electric organ rising up from the floor”.

This is an unattributed retrospective memory, with neither interview date nor remembered year supplied there. It supports how the mechanism could strike a visitor, but cannot establish a particular 1937 audience reaction. The adjacent cinema history resembles R01 and is not independent corroboration. Text inspected; PDF rendering/download was unavailable.

### R03 — contemporary programme evidence, mediated through scholarship

**Conner Rivers Scott, “‘Propaganda for things as they are’? British Newsreels in Everyday Life, c.1920–c.1939,” University of Sheffield PhD, March 2024.** [Thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/35332/2/Final%20Thesis%20White%20Rose%20Copy%20Redacted.pdf#page=49), printed pp. 1, 43–44 / PDF pages 7, 49–50; text inspected.

Minimal passage: “two feature films alongside an assortment of short films”.

Scott examines actual opening programmes: Grantham's State in 1938 combined a Gaumont-British newsreel, cartoon, organ recital and two features; Bolton's Odeon opening in 1937 similarly combined film and live entertainment. References are Harry Sanders Archive SAN4/2/5 and Mass Observation Worktown Box 36-D, image 309. These original archival sheets were **not** independently viewed here. They ground a mixed-bill reconstruction, not a claim that Finchley showed the same cartoon/newsreel. The newsreel's topical moving images are a useful difference from a familiar modern cinema trip; no need to claim everyone loved them.

### R04 — another way the visit differed

**Terence/Terry Gallacher, “This is a movie that ends in the middle…”, recollection published 29 December 2010, reproduced by Luke McKernan on Picturegoing, 2 January 2014.** [Reproduced testimony](https://picturegoing.com/?p=2361). Text inspected; original-blog date/attribution supplied by the reproduction.

Minimal passage: “see the programme through until they reached the point when they entered”.

Gallacher recalls Tottenham and Edmonton cinemas across the 1930s–50s: a repeating programme and visitors arriving when convenient. This is eyewitness reminiscence recorded much later, geographically nearby, not contemporary observation or Finchley-specific evidence. Useful only with an explicit period frame. Do not copy the song lyric quoted on the page or transfer his ticket prices and exact hours to this cinema.

### R05 — campaign actions

**artsdepot, “Timeline – artsdepot's history in context,” undated institutional history, entries through 2025.** [Timeline](https://www.artsdepot.co.uk/timeline/), 2002 and 2004 sections; P01-S01 reopened.

Minimal passage: “masks, banners, and even a performance by Madness at a rally”.

The institution says the council abandoned the change. It does not give the rally's precise date/location, admission terms, songs, lineup or numbers. The source remains sufficient for an attributed recollection of the band appearance; it supplies no basis for “free”, “outside this building” or a reconstructed set-list. Masks and banners can carry the campaign's creativity without invented slogans. Its triumphal interpretation is one participant institution's retrospective account, not a recovered council debate.

### R06 — what was contested

**London Borough of Barnet, “Cabinet 4 April 2012, Public Questions relating to Friern Barnet library,” 4 April 2012.** [Response to Q41, PDF page 22](https://barnet.moderngov.co.uk/Data/Cabinet%20-%202014/201204041900/Agenda/Document%2010.pdf#page=22); P01-S05 reopened and relevant page text inspected.

Minimal passage: “a joint arts and library facility instead of a theatre”.

The council retrospectively confirms considering and rejecting that model in 2002. The wording narrows R05's library-conversion summary: the theatre was the consequential difference. This is a separate institutional position, but neither account supplies the original decision papers. It does not independently verify the rally, nor establish that protest alone caused reversal. Screenshot and direct download failed; no visual inspection claimed.

### R07 — the concrete opening offer

**Sheila Armstrong, “Artsdepot opens,” *The Archer*, November 2004, printed/PDF p. 3.** [Original issue](https://www.the-archer.co.uk/archive/2004/2004NovArcher.pdf#page=3); P01-S04 reopened. The complete page was downloaded, rendered and visually inspected, including the article's continuation down the left column.

Minimal passages: “a studio space for educational and local groups”; “a meeting place for the local community”.

The report covers Duthie's Finchley Society talk on 30 September. Use the two kinds of theatre provision as the clearest explanation of what opened. Local exhibitions and classes supplement it; the café/bar meeting-place statement was expressly a hope. Avoid a catalogue of every room or implying she spoke at the 2002 protest.

### R08–R09 — one present connection

**artsdepot, “artsdepot Creative Circle (for Over 55s),” current listing, 7 September–14 December 2026.** [R08](https://www.artsdepot.co.uk/project/creative-circle/). Minimal passage: “participants can share and develop material together”.

**artsdepot, “This Is Us: Love Through The Seasons,” event dated 1 February 2026, now marked finished.** [R09](https://www.artsdepot.co.uk/event/this-is-us-love-through-the-seasons/). Minimal passage: “Created and performed entirely by participants”.

Both pages were inspected. R09 links work by the writing group, Dance Company and local artists to the stage. These belong to one institutional source family. This supplies a concrete ending about people contributing work, without invented friendships, audience reactions or access promises.

## Omissions, alternative and stopping point

**Omit:** “free” unless independently recovered; detailed funding history and later tenant disputes; opening celebrity lists; redundant intentions; the unverified restrictive-covenant explanation. Also reject the [2015 heritage blog's](https://tallyhoaplacetomeet.wordpress.com/2015/01/15/gaumont-missing-pieces/) claim that the organ served silent films until talkies arrived in 1967: it conflicts with the actual opening films and sound-era chronology. Do not inherit it merely because this was an artsdepot heritage project.

**Useful alternative arrangement:** start with the theatrical protest, briefly explain the theatre/library choice, look back to the cinema's scale and live organ, then return to the present participant performance. This gives the campaign more prominence and makes cinema a revealing comparison. The chronological arrangement is easier to follow and better accommodates a short imagined visit; both retain the requested material without two complete plots.

Searches covered local histories/photo archives, cinema collections, period-programme scholarship, contemporary-press queries and artsdepot/Barnet accounts. Archer's 2002 directory and attempted issues were inaccessible; no independent Madness report or admission record was recovered. The BFI's indexed 1927–39 dossier could not be opened and was not used as evidence. More archive work might refine the performance or cinema programme, but neither gap prevents this attributed, bounded revision. No question requires an owner decision before drafting.
