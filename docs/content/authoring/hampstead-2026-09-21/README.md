## Decisions worth revisiting after the walk

These are observed authoring decisions, not proof that the owner enjoyed the result or new universal rules.

- **Separate route review changed the product.** Imagery showed Merton Lane lacked a continuous footway. The first walking passage moved onto the actual Heath path and the pond pause moved slightly farther along; the story survived without asking the owner for a separate reconnaissance trip.
- **A source can support a good scene without supplying today's exact location.** The 1796 tavern print gives roles, props and an absurd oath. The narration locates the custom in Highgate without pretending the pictured tavern stands at Pond Square.
- **A feature's history does not establish its view.** The pond island was a good research lead but lacked a confirmed sightline. The script uses the visible/general setting and wooden-pipe explanation instead of asking the walker to hunt for it.
- **Specific detail improved the imagined exhibition.** One small elm-and-string sculpture gives the 1942 house exhibition more substance than a list of artists. The independent review retained it while cutting narrator commentary about how interesting the dispute was.
- **A named work needs some content.** The final Keats edit gives a short image from the ode before the current Huxter story, replacing an abstract sentence rather than expanding the stop into a literature lecture.
- **The first complete draft remains available.** Final changes, exact media checks and the owner's eventual responses can be compared with it. Passing a desk review does not settle warmth, pacing or place value.
