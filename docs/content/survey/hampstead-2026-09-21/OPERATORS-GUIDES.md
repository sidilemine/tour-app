# Published walks and guides — Hampstead, with a Highgate start

**21 September 2026 — bounded discovery handoff complete.** [Structured records](operators-guides.json) · [survey protocol](../../AREA-TOUR-SURVEY.md).

## Scope and status

The initial survey covered Hampstead village and accessible southern/western Heath margins, with Belsize Park, Kenwood and Golders Green as nearby comparisons. **During research, Sidi requested a Highgate start.** The final batch adds Highgate village and crossing routes. Station versus village starting point still awaited clarification when this handoff was written. No theme, route or narration is selected here.

The catalogue contains **23 tour/resource records and 115 source-specific place identities**. These are not 23 complete itineraries or 115 verified destinations. Four records are book listings/indexes; some guided tours expose only a theme and endpoints. Place aliases are deliberately not forced together: the coordinator can consolidate, for example, a named tomb and its churchyard without losing what each source actually disclosed.

Actual public descriptions were read through web extraction. Full text directions were available for Richard Jones, A Lady in London, Free Tours by Foot and trainwalkslondon. Mike Blank publishes a complete numbered audio transcript; it was inspected as text, without listening. Paid audio scripts, book chapters and the Metro map were not obtained. No purchase, contact, booking, media reuse, field check or access verification occurred.

## The useful finding

The standard Hampstead pool repeatedly circles **Church Row, Holly Walk, Fenton/Admiral’s House, Well/Flask Walk and Keats House**. That makes them promising candidates, not compulsory stops. A specialist follow-up broadens the possibilities: homes used for refugee assistance, contrasting ideas of domestic life, public recreation and how artists saw the Heath. These can produce a substantially different outing from Clerkenwell’s working-lives tour.

A **Highgate-to-Hampstead walk now needs selection across a landscape**, rather than two complete village tours joined together. For the provisional 75–90-minute allowance, including Highgate, Kenwood, Parliament Hill, Keats, the upper Hampstead village and the Pergola is not a credible starting assumption. Actual routed distance and dwell time must decide what fits.

- Highgate village → eastern Heath lanes/ponds → a chosen public landscape stop → Well Walk/Burgh/Flask/Hampstead. This could ask how the open landscape and nearby houses changed what people could do or imagine.
- Highgate village/Waterlow → cemetery exterior/Swain’s Lane → Parliament Hill → southern Hampstead/Keats/2 Willow Road → finish. This could use changing views and forms of retreat, with different emotional and architectural textures.

These are provisional combinations, not surveyed paths or owner-selected themes. The initial upper-village/Pergola cluster remains in the catalogue for another outing; its presence is not a reason to force a detour now.

## What changes after this survey

1. **Explain motives and mechanisms.** A poet’s residence is only a lead. What did this landscape, household or network allow, and what could the listener see differently afterwards?
2. **Keep the landscape in the experience.** Quiet looking and walking can earn their time. Do not fill the crossing with every available biography.
3. **Choose a few different human stakes.** Health, creative attention, refuge and ordinary recreation offer contrasting possibilities; they need not all become one master theme.
4. **Verify the tempting story before writing it.** Published guides disagree or contain apparent errors. Goldfinger/Fleming revenge, plague-court folklore, precise literary scenes, historic dates and operational claims must be checked against underlying sources.
5. **Make access independent.** A guide’s inclusion does not grant access to gardens, estates, museum interiors or graves. No stopping coordinate or physical direction is established by this catalogue.
