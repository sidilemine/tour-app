## What the survey changes

**The obvious landmarks remain useful, but they are not a sufficient selection method.** The Gate, Green, Smithfield, Charterhouse and churches recur in the inspected material. We should ask which episode or explanation earns each stop rather than retell every associated name.

**Small subjects could provide the best moments.** The gin reliefs, savings bank, goods handling, market workday and Exmouth hot-drinks lamp are promising leads. Some have only a guide's public account so far. They enter targeted research with that status, not a narration script.

**Ordinary work now has a stronger route into the evidence.** The council's flower-making lead connects to an archival collection and Booth notebook catalogue. It could support a worker-centred account rather than another founder biography. Actual documents, activity and experiences still need research. See the [underlying-source check](GAP-CHECK.md#ordinary-work-a-source-trail-behind-the-flower-making-lead).

**The present materially changes the story.** Smithfield's museum opening and continued meat trading have different timelines; pavement works affect route preparation. The Quentin Blake Centre offers a current northern-edge connection. The Green's recent public-space dispute, an Exmouth operator and the NHS clinic listing broaden the present-day pool. Dates and source limits are in [local context](LOCAL-CONTEXT.md#what-changes-the-present-day-picture) and the [gap check](GAP-CHECK.md).

These discoveries support two different combinations: a portrait of working lives, or a developing question about shared provision and pleasure. The [options](TOUR-OPTIONS.md) explain what each would keep and omit. No inference that a less-listed subject is better, original or physically visitable is made.

## Coverage and limits

The core patch runs from Farringdon/Smithfield and St John's through the Green/Close to Exmouth/Spa Fields and the New River Head edge. Angel, Hatton Garden, St Paul's/Barbican and the wider Fleet valley are labelled comparisons. We have not committed to walking the entire research patch.

All six source families received a bounded sweep. Complete council map/key lists and a publisher's 41-point map were inspected alongside partial commercial descriptions, full public route prose and book previews. The withheld chapters, inaccessible full VoiceMap location view, unviewed video leads and Exmouth directory failure are recorded. No paid tour, private script, book purchase, guide contact or on-site visit was required.

The three consequential gaps received follow-up. Broad discovery stops here; further useful work depends on the selected story/route questions. The archive leads do not establish workers' firsthand experiences, and current websites do not establish today's precise pedestrian arrangements. This is comprehensive within the documented public search scope, not a claim to have accessed every tour or all of its content.
