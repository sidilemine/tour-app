## Ordinary work: a source trail behind the flower-making lead

The council leaflet's Woodbridge Chapel/Medical Mission lead offered more than another institutional founder. A targeted search found two inspectable archival routes:

- **`CW-CO-S04` — [AIM25, John Groom's Association for Disabled People, GB 0074 LMA/4305](https://atom.aim25.com/index.php/john-grooms-association-for-disabled-people), description updated January 2013.** The archival biography connects disabled women and girls' artificial-flower work with Sekforde Street and nearby premises. This is an archival description, not inspection of the underlying records or proof of every repeated anecdote.

Groom and supporting mission material. The catalogue was read; the notebooks themselves were not. Its inconsistent overall year label is not used to date the particular interview.

The Children's Homes page was indexed but its direct opening returned an access challenge. It was not used to certify a detailed claim. The first AIM25 URL failed; the canonical URL above opened successfully. No protected records, private personal data, paid archive or imagery was obtained.

**Decision:** retain this as a strong candidate for a story about skilled work, livelihoods and the conditions attached to assistance. Research the workers' activity and agency before making Groom the sole protagonist; do not substitute a sentimental rescue narrative for evidence. Exact premises, tools, materials, pay and first-person experience remain questions for the selected story's research. This closes the discovery question—there is a useful underlying source trail—without pretending to have completed that research.
