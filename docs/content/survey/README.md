# Finchley public walking-tour survey — synthesis

17 September 2026. The first comprehensive public-source pass is complete. It combines commercial, heritage/community and specialist research before the full A/B scripts. This is discovery research, not an assertion that we took these tours or verified their historical claims and access.

## Coverage

The three source-family files retain 50 raw route/resource records. The consolidated [catalogue](catalogue.json) merges five records independently found by two researchers, leaving **45 distinct catalogue entries**. Entries include complete and partial walks, route collections, maps, an estate visit and limited video metadata: this is not 45 complete tour scripts. Name clusters are search aids; similar names have not silently become verified identical locations. The underlying records retain the published points, themes, story angles, sources, sequence/completeness and search logs.

- [Heritage/community](heritage.md): 18 entries, including the three core Roberts landmark maps, council sequences and local green/community walks. [Records](heritage.json).
- [Commercial and platforms](commercial.md): 16 entries, plus six leads. [Records](commercial.json).
- [Specialist themes](thematic.md): 16 entries, five targeted checks. [Records](thematic.json).

Core coverage: North Finchley, Central/Church End, East Finchley, Woodside Park. Nearby Muswell Hill/Fortis Green, woods and longer routes are labelled comparisons. We followed original operators, council PDFs, local-history indices, event archives and specialist sources. Paid/unpublished scripts, withheld intermediate stops and inaccessible video/app steps remain unknown. Further long-distance discovery is unlikely to change these first two tours; pursue retained leads when the corresponding subject becomes useful.

## Decisions for the first two tours

1. **Retain both reviewed themes and the bus-station start/end.** Published North Finchley history already combines transport, entertainment and estate development. Our contribution is the selection, focused stories and walking experience; no claim that we discovered these places first.
2. **A retains five stops:** Tally Ho, Grand Arcade, Torrington, Trinity and artsdepot. The progression is meeting, an everyday shopping passage, a pub audience, residents making music, and a larger shared arts venue. Remove the Stanhope detour as instructed. Reroute the retained sequence before packaging.
3. **B retains six stops plus its walking chapter:** Tally Ho, Trinity, meeting-house sculpture, Moss Hall Crescent, Elephant and artsdepot. Distinguish general estate-to-suburb context on the walk, architectural detail at the villas and continuity of a shared neighbourhood place at the pub. Keep Elephant brief: its best surviving details are indoors and are not promised in this exterior tour.
4. **Correct the Arcade treatment:** local recognition and a campaign supported by Dave Davies do not guarantee permanent preservation. Describe the dated campaign accurately and check current use separately.
5. **Retain Reiniger and Postgate as leads.** A new Waylamp could supply a strong physical animation story, but its decorative map does not establish an exact standing point. Postgate’s nearby address/creation story needs independent checking. Neither is used to add an unverified detour to this release.
6. **Keep East Finchley alternatives separate.** The commercially labelled “North Finchley” Kinks tour starts in East Finchley and reaches Konk by bus. Grange Big Local provides a useful community-tour comparison, not an extra North Finchley stop.

The broader survey supports thematic variety without requiring a wholesale change of location. It has changed our factual checks, strengthened the connective questions and exposed future candidates. Existing owner choices remain primary; rejected memorial and Stanhope detours are not reinstated because another walk lists them.

## What this does and does not settle

Discovery coverage is sufficient to develop the retained A/B versions. Scripts still require paragraph-level evidence and original prose. Routes need desk review, explicit public-exterior limitations and useful manual fallback; the first owner walks will supply actual enjoyment and current physical observations. No survey count substitutes for that evidence.

Reuse begins with these structured records and the shared story/source inputs. The planned authoring database remains a later roadmap stage. Preserve these original branch records when normalising identities further so uncertainty and discovery provenance are not lost.
