# Finchley heritage and community walking-tour survey

Research completed 17 September 2026. This is the heritage-source contribution to the wider public survey; combine it with the commercial, specialist and platform findings. The companion [structured catalogue](heritage.json) stores source provenance, published places, relationships and gaps. No route has been walked or audio listened to during this research.

## What changes our thinking

The existing North Finchley proposals sit within a well-established local-history cluster. Tony Roberts already connects entertainment, transport, vanished buildings and estate development in a much longer route. We should retain the strongest candidates because their particular stories work together, not describe the selection as previously undiscovered. The survey does not overturn Sidi’s rejection of a dedicated Stanhope detour or the John Parr treatment. [Roberts’s landmark trail](https://www.tonero.me.uk/walkmaps/ourwalks120.pdf)

Two useful new leads deserve separate treatment:

- **Reiniger Waylamp:** a newly published 2026 public-art trail gives an actual shadow-making object near Tally Ho, potentially valuable to a future entertainment story. The original schematic places it north of artsdepot but provides no precise address, coordinate or reliable stopping position. Keep it an optional lookout until located; do not add a mandatory GPS stop on this evidence. [Originator’s project](https://www.lightandflight.co.uk/projects/barnet-heritage-trail)
- **Oliver Postgate at 17 Moss Hall Grove:** the new map associates the demolished house with the creation of Ivor the Engine. This may enrich B’s area chapter without another detour. Independently verify the address and chronology before scripting. [Original trail map](https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:eu:df4a79be-97f6-46c0-a1f6-b6b65980142f)

There is no reason to move either compact tour to East Finchley merely because more established itineraries exist there. That is a separate attractive area with a different geography. The first usable A/B versions should keep the authorised footprint and use the broader survey to improve selection and context.

### Wider coverage

The catalogue also records Roberts’s East Finchley Walks, Two Cemeteries, Muswell Hill/Fortis Green, Shady Wood Walk, Hampstead/Highgate circuits, Ally Pally, Six Walks West of Finchley, Byways to Hampstead and the lockdown collection. These have selected principal anchors and their original PDFs, not falsely complete point lists. Much is deliberately nearby comparison material. The lockdown collection contains historical access assumptions and overlapping route variants. [Author’s index](https://www.tonero.me.uk/walksfromfinchley.htm)

The Finchley Society and North Finchley Town Centre refer to Roberts’s work; they are referrals, not separate corroborating tours. The town-centre link uses an older PDF filename. [Society referral](https://www.finchleysociety.org.uk/essential_grid/thirteen-walks-from-finchley/), [town-centre referral](https://northfinchleytowncentre.co.uk/local-stories/story/local-heritage-walks/)

They are recorded as research companions, not invented walking itineraries. [Tally Ho project](https://tallyhoaplacetomeet.wordpress.com/), [newsletter index](https://www.friern-barnethistory.org.uk/newsletter)

## Evidence and access cautions that affect decisions

- Keep historical claim verification separate from discovery. A detailed local walk can contain mistakes. Roberts’s Bohemia chronology is internally awkward, and its treatment of Coppetts Wood’s antiquity differs from the council transcript. These are reasons to check underlying records, not reasons to discard the whole inventory.
- A map’s building marker does not establish a view or permission to enter. Roberts explicitly identifies a house that cannot be seen from the road. Cemetery, churchyard, park and residential-estate access needs current treatment if selected.
- The Waylamps map was downloaded through its public Adobe link and visually inspected. Its decorative scale and approximate positioning do not support precise navigation. The accompanying [field guide](https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:EU:b585115a-c95f-4ae7-ab02-d235a1041020) emphasises creative exploration; its activity language and illustrations are not material to copy into our narration.
- No public reuse licence was established for these scripts, maps or illustrations. Retain links and factual research leads; write our own narration and obtain suitable rights before incorporating media.

## Search and completion statement

Searched area variants for Finchley, North Finchley, Church End/Central, East Finchley and Woodside Park, plus council heritage walks, local-history routes and PDFs. Followed the Finchley Society’s thirteen-walk index to the originator, inspected the three core landmark maps visually, followed church and council map/transcript references, searched the Friern Barnet archive index, and resolved the new Waylamps public files. Exact search families and retrieval limitations are in JSON.

This establishes broad useful heritage coverage for selecting the first A/B tours. Remaining gaps are bounded: peripheral long-walk inventories are partial; the Church End audio transcript was advertised but not retrieved; no separate Woodside Park town-history itinerary was found beyond the North Finchley/Dollis corpus; no audio performance or current physical access was assessed. The council landing page returned 403 directly but its indexed text and public PDFs were available. No paid or unpublished scripts were accessed. These gaps do not require postponing the retained North Finchley routes.
