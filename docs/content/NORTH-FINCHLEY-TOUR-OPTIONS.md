# North Finchley tour options

Discussion proposal 1, 16 September 2026. [Word copy for comments](north-finchley-tour-options-review.docx). Decision and later feedback belong in the [editorial review record](EDITORIAL-REVIEW-RECORD.md), alongside the [living guidance](TOUR-DESIGN-GUIDANCE.md).

## Recommendation

**Start with A if keeping the outing short is the priority. Consider B if the extra ten minutes buys enough variety.** A has the stronger concentration of entertainment stories; B has the better balance of built history, visible art and ordinary community life. These are two concrete proposals for discussion, not selected itineraries or completed E1 variants. My preference for B's variety is an editorial judgement, not a walking observation.

| | A Places people went for a good time | B How a neighbourhood makes itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | Discover the small venues and meeting places behind the high street | Discover what residents built, made and kept alive beyond the shops |
| Six stops | Tally Ho; Grand Arcade; Stanhope cinema; Torrington; Trinity; artsdepot | Tally Ho; Trinity; Quaker sculpture; Moss Hall Crescent; Elephant Inn; artsdepot |
| Pedestrian route estimate | 1.391 km; 18.9 minutes moving | 1.918 km; 26.8 minutes moving |
| Planning allowance including stories | **28–33 minutes** | **37–42 minutes** |
| Main advantage | Compact, lively, recognisable subjects | More varied subjects and more residential streets |
| Main weakness | Repeated High Road pavement, close early stops, two cinema stories | Longer; Elephant story is less distinctive; two legs exceed seven minutes |

Both start and finish by artsdepot/North Finchley bus station, including the walk to the first story at Tally Ho. Times come from actual Valhalla pedestrian responses at a specified 4.5 km/h, plus **8–9 minutes of stationary narration and 2–5 minutes of looking/crossing margin**. Totals are rounded planning ranges, not measured walking times. Simultaneous walking/listening has not been used to make either option appear shorter. A reaches the upper end of the old 20–30-minute goal; B deliberately exceeds it and needs Sidi's preference before selection.

## A Places people went for a good time

**Order:** bus-station approach → 1 Tally Ho → 2 Grand Arcade → 3 Stanhope Picture Theatre → 4 Torrington → 5 Trinity → 6 artsdepot and finish.

1. **Tally Ho — arranging to meet.** Keep the roads and coaching context, then the cyclist's postcard. A light introduction to the corner's role in people's journeys. Use the [revised sample](TALLY-HO-SAMPLE-V2.md); its wording is still for review. About 1½–2 minutes. Evidence S1.
2. **Grand Arcade — a shortcut with a social life.** A small covered shopping passage, with a preservation campaign backed by Dave Davies. Explain why an unassuming place can matter to people who use it. Focus on the passage and small-shop scale, not a lecture about planning. About 1 minute. S2. Current access and visible fabric need checking; local recognition is not a promise of permanent survival.
3. **Stanhope Picture Theatre — cinema before the feature-film evening.** A reproduced advertisement offers continuous programmes and the freedom to arrive and leave when you wish. Use this to evoke a different way of watching films, rather than recite an opening date. About 1–1½ minutes. S3. Candidate location is Jamal House, 14–16 Stanhope Road; the frontage and useful public view remain unverified.
4. **The Torrington — a night out for 80p.** A surviving 1977 advert gives a concrete evening: John Otway and Wild Willy Barrett on the bill, doors at 8.15, admission 80p. Build a supported pub-gig atmosphere around that evidence. About 1½ minutes. S4. Establish the reconstructed scene naturally; do not present imagined incidents or named people's actions as documented events.
5. **Trinity — the orchestra behind an ordinary door.** The church is the normal rehearsal and concert venue for Finchley Symphony Orchestra. Its own account pairs ambitious music with weekly practice and tea-and-cake breaks. A warm present-day story about making culture together. About 1 minute. S5. No entry, concert or audible rehearsal is promised.
6. **Gaumont to artsdepot — a place for audiences again.** Reconstruct the former cinema's social atmosphere, then finish with the campaign and community effort behind today's arts centre. This is the larger public-venue ending, distinct from Stanhope's early continuous shows. About 1½–2 minutes. S6. Exterior narration works without a booking; a café visit is optional and outside the time budget.

**Rhythm and route cost:** the six routed legs, including the initial approach, are 126, 106, 257, 301, 468 and 131 metres. Tally Ho to the arcade is close, and the finale sits near the start. The route goes north for the Torrington and returns along the High Road; Stanhope and Trinity are short side visits. That repeated pavement is an explicit price of the compact selection, not an accidental claim that this is a clean loop. Put quiet on the return leg; do not replay an opening story.

**Before selecting A:** establish whether Grand Arcade is open and useful, whether Stanhope has a worthwhile visible frontage, and whether the close first two stops can use distinct sensible standing areas with the existing arrival policy. Do not tighten trigger thresholds merely to keep six stops. If one fails, replace it or change the outline. Do not count two chapters at one position as two useful physical stops.

## B How a neighbourhood makes itself

**Order:** bus-station approach → 1 Tally Ho → 2 Trinity → 3 Finchley Meeting House → 4 Moss Hall Crescent → 5 Elephant Inn → 6 artsdepot and finish.

1. **Tally Ho — the junction that gathered people.** Same grounded opening as A, establishing the route into the neighbourhood. About 1½–2 minutes. S1.
2. **Trinity — people making music together.** Same orchestra story as A; a small, warm-scale cultural activity before the walk leaves the centre. About 1 minute. S5.
3. **Finchley Meeting House — rubbish becomes a wall sculpture.** Bottle casts and reclaimed slate turn community litter-picking into an artwork. Explain how the materials make the image: discarded containers appear to float among slate waves. An especially strong candidate for noticing something in front of you. About 1½ minutes. S7. The authors invite visitors to see the front-wall work; actual visibility from an appropriate public position still needs checking.
4. **Moss Hall Crescent — the suburb as a promise.** Twelve Victorian villas sit behind a green strip. Read their bays, roofs and separation from the road as evidence of the kind of home being offered when an estate became housing. A place for supported imaginative colour about suburban domestic life, after the factual setting. About 1½ minutes. S8. Stay on public space and avoid turning residents' windows into exhibits; specific original occupants' lives require further evidence.
5. **Elephant Inn — a neighbourhood name survives.** The former Moss Hall Tavern links the vanished estate name to a continuing social place. A brief exterior stop about that continuity, using its documented rebuilding rather than a list of landlords. About 45–60 seconds. S9. This is B's weakest standalone story; keep it only if the building offers a useful visible detail and Sidi likes the subject.
6. **Gaumont to artsdepot — making somewhere to gather.** Return to the bus station area for the shared ending. About 1½–2 minutes. S6.

**Rhythm and route cost:** legs are 126, 211, 603, 487, 217 and 272 metres. The two middle walks each take about 7½ minutes in the router; they offer breathing room but could feel empty. No filler narration is assumed. Most of the western section is a loop through Nether Street and Alexandra Grove; the opening Tally Ho approach and short Moss Hall Crescent visit involve retracing. Ballards Lane remains part of the return, so this is not a traffic-free or fully quiet walk.

**Before selecting B:** decide whether the sculpture and villa story justify the extra time, and whether the Elephant earns a stop. The meeting-house route snaps to the nearby mapped street, not a verified viewing position. Check that small final approach and the sculpture's sightline before using it in narration. This is a story about public art, not a solemn memorial stop.

## Swaps and different story angles

- **More architecture:** St Alban's, Nether Street, could replace Trinity. Taking Stock connects architect Percy Lamb with the building of Westminster Cathedral and describes the tower's carved detail (S10). Start with the detail, then the craft connection. It is close to B's existing western leg, but no replacement route or timing has been calculated. Prefer it if architectural discovery appeals more than amateur music; avoid making a religious-building checklist.
- **More human activity at an existing stop:** at the Torrington, choose the pub-gig evening over an exhaustive celebrity list; at artsdepot, choose collective effort and present use over a demolition lament. These are story alternatives, not extra stops or added walking.
- **More visual and less historical:** the Quaker sculpture is the strongest swap to borrow from B, but moving it into A would require rerouting and more time. It is not a free nearby substitution.
- **A much more distinctive building, later:** the Belarusian wooden church and adjacent library in Woodside Park are a promising separate excursion (S11). They extend beyond this compact brief, access and public sightlines need research, and the church's commemorative context needs careful handling. No extra duration or visit is promised here.
- **Hold rather than pad:** St Michael's school, the synagogue, Northside school and the former tram depot are not promoted just to reach six. This review has not established a stronger visitor-facing story and view for them than the choices above. The John Parr memorial remains excluded following Sidi's review. This is not a claim that these places have no interesting history.

## Questions worth discussing

1. Which **three stories** most make you want to walk: postcard, arcade, early cinema, pub gig, orchestra, sculpture, villas, or neighbourhood pub? Which would you drop?
2. Does B's variety justify roughly **ten extra minutes**, or should we keep working within A's shorter footprint?
3. Would two distinctly treated cinema stops feel like a satisfying thread or repetition? Would the sculpture's environmental subject feel intriguing or too instructional?
4. How much repeated pavement or quiet walking is acceptable when it buys a good story? This proposal makes both costs visible; we have not adopted a general rule yet.

**Next after feedback:** choose a provisional story set, write two short passages that resolve the most important tonal choice, and desk-check retained frontages/approaches. Only then prepare one focused route visit if unresolved access, sightlines or crossing choices still require it. No phone test or outing is requested for this review.

## Route provenance and limits

[Saved requests, responses, waypoint notes and maps](routes/north-finchley-options-v1/README.md) preserve the actual authoring experiment. OSM positions describe public landmarks and approximate street approaches, not the owner's location. No phone trace or private document was sent to a routing provider. Map graphics use OpenStreetMap data, © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); no standard tile-server download was used.

The route returns street/path geometry and sometimes an unhelpful instruction such as “turn left” into a site approach. These are not approved tour directions. Current crossings, surface, gates, sightlines, construction and stop-trigger separation remain unverified. The raw response's lack of time restrictions is not proof of unrestricted arcade access. Both candidate routes lie inside the current map bounds; that alone says nothing about a usable tour.
